# Dream Home visual direction

A bright, private seaside loft experienced from inside, at eye level. The full-height timber-framed sea window on the right wall, its high panes, the ocean horizon beyond, and the light it lets in define every view.

## Palette and character

Warm-white plaster `#f1ece2`, oak floor and frame `#c9a67e` / `#a77950`, sea-blue textiles `#6f9aab`, oatmeal linen `#ece5d6`, one clay accent `#b97458` used sparingly (a throw, a cushion, a book), a soft grey-green `#8fa39f` inside the bed alcove, charcoal-blue gaming equipment `#263e50`. Natural-wood tea furniture avoids red-and-gold decoration.

Warmth comes from light and material, not from tinting the image. Daylight is one-sided: a warm sun and a cool, shadowless sky wash both enter through the sea window, with a low bounce from the floor, so surfaces turn from sunlit to shaded across the room. Lamps add small warm pools: bedside, under the canopy, on the console beneath the loft, on the desk, over the reading chair, a paper lantern over the tea table and a paper globe in the tall space. At night they carry the room. Wood has grain and staggered boards, cloth has a weave, plaster is faintly clouded, furniture sits in soft contact shade. Colours are rendered with a neutral tone curve so whites stay white.

Signs of someone living here are few and specific: a turned-down duvet and a dropped throw, a book left face down, slippers, a warm cup with a thread of steam, a coat and sun hat by the door.

## Composition

An 18.5-wide room with a solid rear wall, a left wall and desk-side window onto pines, and a full-height right sea window. The mezzanine is 8.05 wide and 3.9 deep: compact around the tea table with chair clearance, while the window side remains double height.

Shelter without enclosure: the bed meets the real rear wall inside an alcove whose linen canopy sits at the height of the loft floor, continuing that line across the room. An open timber screen stands between the bed and the rest of the room; the sea side has nothing in the way. No post stands in the sleeping area, the canopy hangs from two slim rods. The reading chair gets the same treatment at a smaller scale: a low bookshelf at its back, the window in front. The tea table sits with the bookcase behind and the railing and the sea ahead.

The gaming desk faces the room with the chair backed toward the left wall, clear of the stair. An L-shaped stair starts beside the central aisle and enters through a gap in the loft railing. Bookcases on the upper rear and lower left walls connect the two levels. A ceiling and a front wall with the entry door close the room for someone standing in it and disappear in the outside views.

Outside the glass: a weathered deck, pale shore with dune grass, sea to the horizon, a far headland with a lighthouse that blinks at dusk and night. The sky has slow cloud, a sun or moon that matches the light in the room, and stars. The outside world fades into the page colour at its edges so the house still reads as a diorama from outside.

## Interaction

The visitor arrives standing inside the door. Dragging turns the head; clicking the floor walks there; the stair can be climbed. Clicking the desk, the bed, the reading chair or the tea table sits the visitor down at that place with a view chosen for it, and a quiet card names the place and offers to stand up. A short blink replaces any move that would otherwise fly through furniture or a floor.

Open front and From above restore the cut-away orbit views. Day/sunset/night, rain, curtains, and lamps remain available; bookcases and the rolling TV keep their detail panels, and the TV can move between bed and window. Toolbar controls mirror clickable scene targets. The title gives way once the visitor starts to look around, and returns in the outside views.
